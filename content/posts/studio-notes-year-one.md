A year ago we left good jobs to start a two-person studio. Here are some honest notes from the first twelve months.

## What went right

**Saying no early.** We turned down three projects in the first quarter that were "AI for the sake of AI." It felt terrifying at the time. It meant we had space for the work that became our best case studies.

**Fixed-scope discovery sprints.** A short, fixed-price first engagement lets clients try us with low risk, and lets us find out whether an idea holds up before anyone commits to a big build.

**Writing in public.** Almost half our inbound leads mention something we wrote. The posts that did best were the most specific ones.

## What went wrong

**Underestimating handover.** Our first project ended with a working system and a client team that didn't fully understand it. Now we budget 15% of every project for documentation, pairing and a recorded walkthrough.

**Too many tools.** We tried a new framework on nearly every project. Fun, but slow. We now have a boring default stack and only deviate with a good reason.

## What's next

More open source, a few more long-term partners, and maybe — carefully — a third person. If you're curious about working together, [say hello](/hire/).
