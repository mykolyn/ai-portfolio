When I tell people I'm a forward deployed engineer, I usually get one of two reactions: a blank look, or "so… a solutions engineer?" Close, but not quite. Here's what the job looks like day to day.

## The one-sentence version

An FDE is a software engineer whose codebase happens to live inside a customer's problem. I'm accountable for the outcome: not the demo, not the proof of concept, but the thing actually working for the people who use it.

> [!pull] The demo is where the job starts, not where it ends.

## A typical week

- **Monday:** on site with a customer, shadowing the team who'll use what I'm building. I take notes on everything they do that isn't in the spec.
- **Tuesday–Wednesday:** heads-down building. Integrations into their systems, the agent or retrieval layer, and an eval set made from what I saw on Monday.
- **Thursday:** a working demo on their real data. Usually half of it is wrong in interesting ways, which is the point.
- **Friday:** fix what broke, write up what I learned, and send product feedback to the core team so the next customer doesn't hit the same wall.

## What makes it different

**You own the outcome, not the ticket.** Nobody hands an FDE a fully specified task. Part of the job is working out what the task should be.

**Range beats depth (mostly).** In one day I might debug an SSO integration, tune a retrieval pipeline, and explain evaluation results to a VP. You need to be credible in all three rooms.

**You're the product team's eyes.** The most valuable thing I ship is often not code. It's a clear write-up of where the product broke in the real world.

## Who it's for

If you like engineering but get restless without seeing who uses your work, and you'd rather be on a customer call than in a planning meeting, it might be the job for you. It's certainly the job for me.
