The short story

I started out as a software engineer who kept ending up in customer meetings. Somewhere along the way I noticed that the hardest part of shipping AI wasn’t the model. It was everything around it: messy data, unwritten workflows, the integration nobody scoped, and the person who has to trust the output enough to act on it.

Forward deployed engineering is the job that owns all of that. I scope with the customer, prototype on their real data within days, harden it into production, and stay close enough to see whether it actually gets used. Then I bring what I learned back to the product team so the next deployment is easier.

Outside of work I write about what I’m learning, tinker on open-source eval tooling, and overthink loading states.

How I work

  1. Start in the customer’s chair.

    Before writing code I shadow the workflow. The real requirements are rarely in the ticket.

  2. Evals before vibes.

    If I can’t measure “better,” I can’t ship it. Every deployment starts with a test set, not a demo.

  3. Prototype in days.

    A rough thing on real data beats a polished plan. Feedback early, polish later.

  4. Leave it owned.

    Documented, tested and handed over, so the customer’s team can run it without me.
